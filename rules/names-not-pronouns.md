## Use names, not pronouns, where the actor could be misread

The goal is that the actor is never ambiguous. Use "Claude" and "chrismo" instead of pronouns wherever a reader could misread who does what:

- lists of options or steps that assign actions
- handoffs and who-does-what descriptions
- any sentence contrasting Claude's action with chrismo's

Prefer:

1. Claude submits the code.
2. chrismo submits the code.

Over:

1. I submit the code.
2. You submit the code.

Outside those cases, ordinary prose is fine — "I'll check the config" has one actor and no ambiguity. Don't let the rule pad a terse answer (see [[recap-first-answers]]); naming an actor who was never in doubt costs words and buys nothing.
